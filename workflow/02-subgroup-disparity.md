# Iteration 02 — Subgroup performance disparity

## Business analysis

**Driver, quoted from their own open requisition** (Director, Post Market Quality):

> "Establish real-world performance monitoring for AI/ML-enabled device functions, with
> predetermined thresholds for accuracy and **subgroup performance**, and a defined escalation
> path when drift or **performance disparity** is detected."

That is a specification, and iteration 01 does not satisfy it. Standardisation gives the
*average* effect after adjusting for mix. A release can be neutral on average and still hurt
one subgroup badly — the averaging is precisely what hides it.

**Why this matters beyond compliance.** The failure mode in the MAUDE record is a false
negative on a vessel the model over-segmented. If that failure concentrates in, say,
high-calcium cases at office-based sites, then the patients absorbing the risk are a specific
identifiable population, and "our overall rate is fine" is not an answer.

**What we can and cannot subgroup on.** The spine carries no demographics — no age, sex,
race, or insurance status — by design, because it carries no patient identity at all. So this
is *clinical and operational* subgroup analysis, not demographic equity analysis. Those are
different things and conflating them would be dishonest. Real demographic subgroup monitoring
would require a governed join to a source that does hold those attributes, with its own
privacy review. Flagged as out of scope, deliberately, not overlooked.

Available subgroup axes: calcium band, motion band, stent presence, detector generation,
site class (office vs hospital), BMI band.

## Architecture

**The problem that decides the design: multiplicity.**

Six subgroup axes × several levels each × three releases is on the order of 50 comparisons.
At α = 0.05 you expect roughly two "significant" findings from pure chance. Ship that and
Quality receives a steady drip of phantom disparities, stops reading them, and the one real
signal arrives into an inbox nobody trusts. This is the same failure as iteration 00's raw
lift threshold, one level up.

**Decision: Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate control at q = 0.10.**

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| No correction | Rejected. ~2 false positives per run, monitor dies of noise. |
| Bonferroni | Rejected. Controls family-wise error, but at ~50 tests the per-test threshold is 0.001 and real disparities on small subgroups become undetectable. Too conservative for a *screening* tool. |
| **Benjamini–Hochberg FDR** | **Chosen.** Controls the expected *proportion* of false discoveries among flagged findings, which is the right target for a monitor whose output is "investigate these". q = 0.10 means roughly one in ten flagged items is expected to be a false lead — acceptable for triage, and stated openly rather than implied. |

**Second decision: report an effect size and a confidence interval, never a bare p-value.**
A p-value on 5,000 cases will flag a 0.3 pp difference nobody should act on. The escalation
rule is conjunctive: **FDR-significant AND disparity ratio ≥ 1.5 AND the smaller arm has ≥ 30
cases.** All three, or it is not escalated.

**Third decision: Wilson score intervals, not normal approximation.** Subgroup arms get small,
and the normal interval produces bounds outside [0,1] and undercovers at extreme rates.
Wilson behaves at the edges. This is a small thing that a reviewer who knows statistics will
check.

**Where it lives:** `spine/metrics.py` again, so API, MCP and Cube share one definition.

**Schema change required:** `stratum` is a composite string, so the individual axes are not
queryable on their own. Adding `calcium_band`, `motion_band`, `bmi_band` as first-class
columns on the spine. Cheap, and it keeps the disparity code from parsing a display string —
parsing a formatted label to recover data is a smell that always comes back.

## Iterate → next

Disparity monitoring answers "who is being hurt". It does not answer "what do we do about the
5–15% of cases the ingest gate rejects outright" — cases that never reach an analyst, never
generate revenue, and send a clinician away empty-handed after waiting. That is iteration 03.
